That is a strong next expansion. Product Manager behavioral interviews would complement the engineering roles well because PM interviews test a different center of gravity: **customer judgment, product sense, prioritization, influence without authority, ambiguity, trade-offs, and business impact**.

For the next round, I would add PM-specific behavioral question categories such as:

| PM competency               | Example behavioral questions                                                                               |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Customer obsession          | “Tell me about a time you changed your product direction after learning something from customers.”         |
| Prioritization              | “Describe a time you had to choose between two important product opportunities.”                           |
| Influence without authority | “Tell me about a time you convinced engineering, design, or leadership to support your product direction.” |
| Ambiguity                   | “Describe a time you had to define a product from an unclear problem.”                                     |
| Trade-offs                  | “Tell me about a time you had to balance user experience, technical feasibility, and business goals.”      |
| Data-informed decisions     | “Describe a time when data changed your mind.”                                                             |
| Failed launch               | “Tell me about a product or feature launch that did not meet expectations.”                                |
| Stakeholder conflict        | “Describe a time when sales, engineering, design, or leadership disagreed with your roadmap.”              |
| Strategy                    | “Tell me about a time you developed or changed a product strategy.”                                        |
| Execution                   | “Describe a time you took a product from idea to launch under difficult constraints.”                      |
| Ethics and trust            | “Tell me about a time you decided not to ship something because it was wrong for users.”                   |
| Working with engineering    | “Describe a time you had to negotiate scope, timeline, or technical debt with engineering.”                |

A useful book structure would be to show how the same behavioral theme differs across roles:

| Theme          | Software Engineer                | Engineering Manager               | Product Manager                       | Senior Tech Leader                             |
| -------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Ambiguity      | Clarifies requirements           | Helps team move forward           | Defines the problem and opportunity   | Sets direction across the org                  |
| Conflict       | Handles peer disagreement        | Mediates team conflict            | Aligns stakeholders without authority | Resolves executive-level trade-offs            |
| Failure        | Learns from a bug or missed task | Improves team process             | Learns from a failed launch           | Changes organizational systems                 |
| Prioritization | Chooses what to work on first    | Balances team capacity            | Chooses product bets                  | Allocates portfolio-level investment           |
| Customer focus | Understands user impact          | Builds customer-aware team habits | Owns customer and market insight      | Connects technology strategy to customer value |

For Product Managers specifically, I would include one important distinction:

**Engineering interviews often ask: “How did you build it?”**
**PM interviews often ask: “Why was it worth building?”**

That contrast will help readers understand why PM behavioral examples should not sound like engineering examples with different job titles.
